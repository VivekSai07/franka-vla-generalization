### 1. The written success definition cannot be met by an object taller than the bin is deep

Success requires the object's centre to be inside the inner box **and below the wall top (0.07 m)**. A cereal
box is 15 cm tall and the bin floor is 1 cm up: standing on the floor its centre of mass is at 0.0855 m. The
expert puts it there, upright, centred (xy margin 0.10 m), gripper open and clear — and the definition calls it a
failure. The same holds for the milk carton (centre 0.072 m) and for the bottle. Seen directly on the first
failure sheet (cereal row): the box stands in the blue bin with its top well above the walls.

- Every success among the tall objects is an object that **fell over** (tilt > 30° in all 35 successes: milk 20,
  bottle 14, cereal 1). Better grasps make milk score worse: after the face-grasp fix milk moved from 21 to 20
  successes because more cartons now stay upright.
- It does not depend on the controller: robosuite's OSC controller, same attempts, also leaves milk, bottle and
  cereal standing (`not_in_bin` 27, 39 and 26 of 50).
- `metrics.placement` records where the object really rests, as a **diagnostic that is not in the verdict**: bottom
  height, tilt, xy margin, resting on the bin floor. By it, **241 of 300 attempts (80.3%) end with the object resting
  inside the bin**, and **198 of the 199 attempts that start 0.285 m or more from the robot base do** (the exception
  is the lemon above). The 59 attempts that do not are the 58 near-base grasp failures and that lemon.
- Release height (0.04 and 0.01 m over the wall) does not change whether a tall object stands (below); making the
  objects fall over to satisfy the definition was not tried, because that would reward dropping them.

The success definition and the thresholds are unchanged. Whether to amend the definition is the reviewer's decision.

### 2. The near-base part of the start region is outside what the expert's path can reach

All 58 near-base grasp failures are attempts whose start is within about 0.28 m of the robot base, and the
boundary is sharp: for the short objects (can, bread, lemon), **117 of 120 attempts that start 0.265 m or more
from the base succeed, against 12 of 30 closer in.** About 20% of the start region (x 0.22–0.40, |y| ≤ 0.12) is
closer than 0.265 m. The cause, read from the traces:

- On those attempts joint 4 reaches its fold limit (−3.0718 rad) during the approach and stays there; the hand ends
  4–5 cm farther out than the target and closes on air (a failed lift check, `reason="grasp"`; on the failure sheet
  the can row's gripper camera sees nothing between the fingers).
- It is not the hand being unable to be there: a numerical IK with the real Panda limits (`reach_study.py`) finds a
  vertical hand at 0.155 m from the base for heights up to 0.225 m. It is the **path**: the expert's approach is a
  straight Cartesian line from the home pose (z 0.58) to a hover point 0.15 m above the grasp; near the base that line
  passes at z 0.30–0.35 and radius < 0.27 m, where the same study says a vertical hand needs at least 0.235 m
  (z = 0.30) and farther at greater heights. Differential IK with a posture bias towards home cannot recover once the
  joint saturates.
- Lowering the hover height (0.10, 0.08, 0.06 m, on top of grasp depth 0.05) moved the whole plan by 175, 180, 176
  against 177: not the lever. A path that descends first and then moves in, or an approach that is not a straight
  line from home, probably is. Neither is on the tuning list and neither was built.
- B3's regions started at radius ≥ 0.28 m; the props spec starts 0.22 m from the base, a region the cube expert never
  had to reach.

### 3. A defect in the first expert, found by the gate and fixed

The first version treated a footprint whose long and short extents were within 10% as "round" and kept the home
hand yaw. A milk carton is square (4 × 4 cm): at a diagonal yaw the fingers closed on two corners and the carton
squeezed out (4 of milk's 17 grasp failures, all at yaws 47–65°; seen on the failure sheet). The plan names this class
of error (Review Focus 3, "period"). Fix `3e19c01`, test-first: footprint widths along the body axes and their diagonals
decide "round"; among equally narrow faces the one needing the smaller hand rotation is taken. The new test failed
against the old planner. This is a defect fix, not an item on the tuning list; it is reported as a deviation.

### 4. Replay from float32 actions drifted until the expert applied the float32 action

The first replay test failed: replaying the recorded float32 actions kept the arm within 1.8e-7 rad but moved the
can's pose by 7.5 mm, because the expert had commanded the float64 target and recorded its float32 rounding. The
expert now applies the float32 value it records, and replay is exact (error 0.0 on all 300 attempts).

## What the expert does per object

Grasp height is `min(0.03, height/2)` below the object's top; the hand frame sits 0.105 m above that point. Hand
orientation is the home orientation turned about world z so the fingers close across the footprint's narrowest face.

| object | bounding box (cm) | footprint rule | hand z at grasp (m) |
|---|---|---|---|
| can | 5.2 × 5.2 × 8 | round: home yaw | 0.155 |
| bread | 4.8 × 4.0 × 4.8 | closes across the 4.0 side | 0.129 |
| lemon | 7 × 4 × 4.3 | closes across the 4.0 side | 0.127 |
| milk | 4.0 × 4.0 × 14.4 | square: the nearer face (hand yaw within ±45°) | 0.219 |
| bottle | 5.5 × 5.9 × 16 | round: home yaw | 0.235 |
| cereal | 10 × 3 × 15 | closes across the 3.0 side | 0.225 |

Phases (s): approach 2.0 (hover 0.15 m above the grasp), descend 1.5, close 1.0, lift 1.5 (object bottom 0.05 m over
the wall top, verified by contact and a rise of at least half the lift), move 2.5, lower 1.5 (bottom 0.02 m over the
wall top), open 1.0, retract 1.5, home 2.5; 452 ticks. Carry and release targets are measured from the object in the
hand. A successful episode needs the 30-tick streak to still hold at the last tick.

**No change was adopted.** Of the 37 attempts whose outcome changed under depth 0.05, 18 went from "standing" to
"success" and 7 the other way, all tall objects: the gain is whether a bottle or carton happens to tip over, which the
definition rewards and which is not what the task wants. On the 21 can, bread and lemon attempts that failed near the
base (the failing-set re-run, before the yaw fix), no knob on the list helped except marginally: 0 of 21 under most,
and at best durations ×1.5 (bread 3 of 5, lemon 1 of 11) and, with one lemon each, durations ×0.75 and friction 0.8.
Gate 2 therefore runs the expert as it was after the defect fix, with every constant at its spec value.

## What was surprising

- A perfect placement fails the written definition (finding 1); the worse the grip, the better the score.
- The near-base failures are a path problem, not a reach problem; lowering the hover did not help.
- A parallel probe of 44 workers needs more RAM than the machine has (each holds a few GB of models, 3.7 GB resident)
  and stalled; the same job with 30 workers on contiguous chunks finished in 1 min 45 s. Killing the foreground
  command did not stop the background shell loop that launched it.
- `Ran 0 tests … OK` appeared when a test run started right after the file was written: check the test count.

## What this does not show

- No policy was trained; nothing here says how well X-VLA can learn these tasks.
- Each attempt is one run at one seed; the rates for toppling objects are noisy (the same knob moves them by ±10).
- Only the written definition decides the verdict; the diagnostic figures are labelled as such and were chosen after
  seeing the failures.
- Friction, contact parameters and masses are the cube's contact values and the plan's masses; whether they are
  realistic for these objects was not examined.

Decision: no-go

## What G1 answers

1. **Does A1 hold G0's axes 1-4?** Lighting, background and spatial: yes. Distractors: no (drop 9.2, upper bound 15.6; `five` 17.4). Held does not mean the
   same thing everywhere: spatial and lighting levels are inside the training ranges.
2. **Does it recover better on axis 5 (disturbance)?** Yes, clearly (drop 15.7 against 38.6; recovered 103/133 against 73/133), but it does not hold it
   (`move` 11.6 [21.7], `drop` 19.7 [28.2]). No recovery demonstration was in the data.
3. **Does it beat A2 where it should (D10)?** Criterion "held and beats A2": lighting and spatial at the primary checkpoint (plus background at 101,520).
   Distractors and disturbance beat A2 strongly but are not held.
4. **Did randomization cost nominal performance?** No measurable cost: -1.4 points on G0's nominal trials, -2.1 on M0's comparison tier, intervals include 0.
5. **G2's start condition (an axis-5 level not held) holds**: both disturbance levels fail the bar. Not started; the user decides.

## What G1 cannot say

- Which randomized factor produced which gain (a bundle by design), or whether spatial is fixed by the table-height range, the outer ring, the +-10 degree
  home jitter or the volume of states. The joint-space reach hypothesis of G0 is consistent with the result but not tested.
- Anything outside the trained ranges: spatial and lighting levels beyond them, camera-pose changes, categories (G3), physical parameters.
- Whether more exposure helps. The aggregate is flat from 101,520 to 126,900 but the learning rate is at its floor there; distractors (`five`) and
  `low_angle` are where more exposure could matter, so a continuation from 126,900 would be a separate one-variable run (new contract, not `--resume`),
  not started.
- Training-seed variance (one seed per arm), and any real-world transfer.
